## Delivering in the Life Sciences industry

Life Sciences projects require risk-based execution because outputs may support regulated products, quality processes, clinical or laboratory decisions, manufacturing, distribution, or patient-facing services.

### Core principles

- **Patient and user safety:** make potential harm visible in requirements, risk assessment, testing, release, and escalation.
- **Product quality:** protect the intended quality, reliability, and performance of products and processes.
- **Data integrity:** ensure records remain attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original or verified, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, and available as applicable.
- **Traceability:** link approved needs to design, configuration, testing, defects, decisions, release, and acceptance.

| Phase | Minimum practice |
|---|---|
| Proposal | Identify regulatory, quality, validation, security, privacy, records, audit, and environment needs. |
| Mobilization | Confirm intended use, classification, procedures, training, access, and approval roles. |
| Requirements | Use approved testable requirements, risk assessment, and traceability. |
| Build | Apply version control, review, configuration standards, environment controls, and secure development. |
| Validation | Use approved protocols, representative conditions, retained results, failure investigation, and traceability. |
| Release | Obtain required quality, business, security, privacy, and operational approvals. |
| Change and retirement | Assess impact, regression, revalidation, records retention, migration, archival, and disposal. |

> Stop and obtain specialist direction when classification is unclear, required evidence is missing, sensitive data may be exposed, or release is requested before required approvals.
