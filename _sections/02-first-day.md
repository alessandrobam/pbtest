## Your first-week outcome

By the end of the first week, you should be able to explain what the project must deliver, why it matters, who decides, where authoritative records live, what is currently at risk, and what needs attention next.

### Day 1 — Get connected

- Meet your manager, onboarding partner, Delivery Lead, outgoing Project Manager, Abbott sponsor, and workstream leads.
- Request access to collaboration tools, project repositories, plans, financial tools, required training, and approved Abbott systems.
- Learn the Abbott business area, users, sites, products, processes, markets, project tier, current phase, and delivery method.
- Identify the quality, commercial, finance, privacy, security, and regulatory contacts that apply to the work.

### Days 2–5 — Understand the project

| Review | Questions to answer |
|---|---|
| Purpose | What problem are we solving, who benefits, and how is success measured? |
| Contract | What did we promise, exclude, assume, price, and commit to deliver? |
| Plan | What is complete, late, blocked, next, or at risk? Is the forecast credible? |
| Governance | Who owns, decides, approves, contributes, and needs to be informed? |
| Risk | What could affect safety, quality, scope, cost, schedule, data, or operations? |
| Life Sciences | Is the work regulated or quality-relevant, and which controls apply? |
| Commercials | Are effort, cost, revenue, margin, billing, and changes aligned? |

### First 30 days — Take ownership

1. **Understand:** complete the handover and publish immediate priorities.
2. **Validate:** confirm the plan, forecast, commercial position, risks, and controls.
3. **Align:** agree the project narrative with Abbott stakeholders.
4. **Improve:** address the most valuable gaps and establish a sustainable operating rhythm.

> Before accepting a handover, record unclear commitments, missing evidence, overdue decisions, access blockers, financial exposure, and material risks. Agree owners and dates.
